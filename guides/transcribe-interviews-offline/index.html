Why keep recordings on your computer

Consent forms and ethics approvals usually say where interview recordings are kept and who may process them. A cloud transcription service is one more party that receives the audio and processes it on its own servers. Before you upload anything, check what your approval and your data management plan allow.

You may not need to upload at all. Whisper, the open speech-recognition model, runs on an ordinary laptop. After a one-time model download it needs no internet connection. The rest of this page shows the free route step by step, exactly as it ran on 8 November 2026.

The free route, step by step

You need Python 3.10 or newer and about 2 GB of disk space for the model. Make a folder, put your recording in it as interview.wav (MP3, M4A and most other formats work too), and save this script beside it as transcribe.py:

import sys

from faster_whisper import WhisperModel

model = WhisperModel("large-v3-turbo", device="cpu", compute_type="int8")
segments, info = model.transcribe(sys.argv[1], vad_filter=True)
print(f"language: {info.language}, audio: {info.duration:.0f} s")
for s in segments:
    m, sec = divmod(int(s.start), 60)
    h, m = divmod(m, 60)
    print(f"[{h:02d}:{m:02d}:{sec:02d}] {s.text.strip()}")

It uses faster-whisper, a fast build of Whisper that runs on the processor of any Mac, Windows or Linux computer. large-v3-turbo is a faster version of Whisper's largest model; vad_filter=True skips silence. Each line gets the time it starts, in the [hh:mm:ss] form MAXQDA, ATLAS.ti and QualCoder read.

The install error you will hit today

Installed the obvious way, it fails as soon as it opens the recording:

$ python3 -m venv naive-env
$ naive-env/bin/pip install faster-whisper
$ naive-env/bin/python transcribe.py interview.wav
...
TypeError: open() got an unexpected keyword argument 'metadata_errors'

faster-whisper 1.2.1, the current release, does not work with PyAV 17 or newer, the library it uses to read audio. Installing PyAV below version 17 next to it fixes that. On Windows, the commands use whisper-env\Scripts\pip and whisper-env\Scripts\python instead.

$ python3 -m venv whisper-env
$ whisper-env/bin/pip install faster-whisper "av<17"
$ whisper-env/bin/pip list | grep -E '^(faster-whisper|av|ctranslate2) '
av                16.1.0
ctranslate2       4.8.5
faster-whisper    1.2.1
$ time whisper-env/bin/python transcribe.py interview.wav > interview.txt

$ head -6 interview.txt

Proof that it works offline

On a Mac, sandbox-exec can run a command with all network access denied. curl cannot reach Hugging Face inside it (exit code 6), and the transcription still runs. HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 tells the library not to look for a newer model online.

$ sandbox-exec -p '(version 1)(allow default)(deny network*)' curl -s -m 5 https://huggingface.co -o /dev/null; echo $?
6
$ HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 sandbox-exec -p '(version 1)(allow default)(deny network*)' \
    whisper-env/bin/python transcribe.py first-minute.wav
language: en, audio: 60 s
[00:00:02] Hello. Hello, how are you? Hi. Shall we stop? Yeah, okay. Hello. Good morning, sir. How can I help you this morning?
[00:00:12] Hi. I've just had some diarrhoea for the last three days, and it's been affecting me. I need to stay close to the toilet, and yeah, it's been affecting my day-to-day activities.

What the free route leaves to you

Who said what. Whisper writes words, not speakers. In the run above, the doctor's and the patient's words share lines: "Hello. Hello, how are you? Hi. ... Good morning, sir. How can I help you this morning?" is both of them. Adding speakers yourself usually means pyannote. Its speaker models on Hugging Face are gated: you need an account, must accept the model's conditions, and must pass an access token to the code (checked 8 November 2026).

Names. The transcript holds every name, place and organisation anyone said. To share it with co-authors, an archive or a coding team, you replace them, and the same person must get the same tag in every interview. That takes a key of who is who. Archives such as the UK Data Service advise keeping that log separate from the anonymised files. While the key exists, the data are pseudonymised, not anonymous.

Your analysis software. The script's [hh:mm:ss] lines suit MAXQDA, ATLAS.ti and QualCoder when you link the recording. NVivo for Windows imports a table with a Timespan and a Content column. NVivo for Mac wants Name: at the start of each turn, timestamps without brackets, and no other colons in the text.

Hard audio. On a noisy phone line the silence filter can throw away most of the speech: in our own tests it kept 161 seconds of a 48-minute call. If a transcript is much shorter than the recording, run it again with vad_filter=False.

If you would rather not write code, three free programs run Whisper on your own computer with a window to click in (as their own pages described them in November 2026):

Or let one command do all of it

Local Transcripts is our tool for this job. It runs on your computer in the same way and adds the parts above: who said what, a pseudonymised copy with the key kept apart, and files for MAXQDA, NVivo (Windows and Mac), ATLAS.ti and QualCoder. Here it is on the same consultation, on Apple silicon:

The full results, the weak cases included, are on its page. It costs $29 once, or $99 for a research group of five.

Before you transcribe

  • Check what your consent form, ethics approval and data management plan say about storage and processing.
  • Name recordings by participant code, not by name: the file name ends up in every transcript and export.
  • Keep the recordings, the full transcripts and any key on an encrypted disk, apart from what you share.
  • Read every transcript against the recording before you quote from it. Whisper mishears names and numbers.
  • Before sharing, search the shareable copy for the names you know were said.
