From the labs

Of 135 releases from 13 labs, 3 change your work or are worth trying, and 11 are good to know.

Changes your work (1)

  1. Ambiguous Fits: The Rashomon Effect and Challenges in Scaling Law Estimation
    Google DeepMind · paper · 25 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.34514
    Shows that the usual scaling-law fit (local optimisation from a grid of starts) has many near-best fits whose extrapolations diverge at scale, and gives a branch-and-bound fit that guarantees a near-optimal loss and maps the whole range of implied curves. Worth rerunning your own scaling fits this way; the abstract does not say whether code is released.

Worth trying (2)

  1. Efficient Quantization Training with Stochastic Rounding via Structured Subspace Randomness
    Google Research · paper · 27 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.36587
    S3R-EC shares one stochastic-rounding threshold per token and subtracts the resulting mean error, matching independent stochastic rounding's validation loss in LLM pretraining while making full INT8 steps 1.24-1.32x faster than BF16. The results are on TPU v5p; the abstract reports no GPU numbers, so check whether it carries over to your setup.
  2. Agentic Backdoors via Pretraining Poisoning
    Anthropic · paper · 27 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.37741
    Poisoning 0.1% of a pretraining corpus planted a backdoor that makes a cleanly post-trained CLI agent run attacker-chosen commands, and success rose with model size (about 92% at 4B, still positive at 0.0005%). If you assemble web pretraining mixtures, worth an afternoon with their released code to see what your filters would catch.

Good to know (11)

  1. Post-Training Large Language Models with LLMON
    IBM Research · paper · 28 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.38120
    IBM post-trained Granite-4.1 3B and 8B (SFT then RL) on a 4.6B-token corpus that separates instructions from data, cutting indirect prompt-injection success from 53-100% to 0.02-5.6% while keeping general capabilities. A data format to know for instruction tuning; the abstract does not say whether the corpus is released.
  2. ThinkBit: Search Codewords, Recalibrate Scales, Select by Reasoning
    Microsoft Research · paper · 26 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.34886
    Two-bit weight quantization built to keep reasoning: Qwen3-1.7B scores 46.7 on MATH-500 against 30.0 for EfficientQAT, and a 3.74 GB 14B model scores 94.8 against 97.5 in bf16. Relevant if you evaluate compressed models, since the authors note perplexity and multiple-choice scores say little about reasoning.
  3. Multi-State Linear Attention with Generalized Second-Order Routing
    Google DeepMind · paper · 27 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.37645
    MERLIN, a multi-state linear attention that routes each query on the mean and variance of every segment's keys instead of the mean alone. Context for efficient long-sequence architectures.
  4. The State and Fate of Multilingual, Contextual Evaluation in the NLP World
    Microsoft Research · paper · 27 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.36990
    An audit of 51 multilingual benchmarks (242 datasets, 219 languages) finds coverage wide but thin, and 56% of dataset-language instances translated from English. Good to know if you report multilingual results, as for the Bloom-style models in your library.
  5. Rethinking Verification in Agent Harnesses
    Alibaba · paper · 25 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.34577
    Compares 17 verification designs across seven coding models on Terminal-Bench and SWE-bench Verified; an independent critic adds 5.9-9.5 points on Terminal-Bench at 1.13-1.25x cost. Shows how much harness choices alone move coding scores, which matters when you compare models on these benchmarks.
  6. Co-Design Bench: Multimodal Collaborative Coding Evaluation for Agentic Models
    Alibaba · paper · 26 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.35138
    A coding-agent benchmark of 273 tasks from real user sessions with 2,331 hidden acceptance criteria; the best of ten endpoints fully satisfies only 26.4% of tasks. Context for code evaluation; it is graded by an LLM judge-then-refute protocol rather than gold code.
  7. Blind Spots: Where Small Language Models Fall Behind Frontier Models on Terminal Tasks
    Microsoft Research · paper · 28 Oct 2026 · arXiv:2610.39006
    Splits the small-versus-frontier model gap on terminal tasks into solvability, verification and confidence: repeated sampling narrows the first, but small models stay worse at picking their own correct runs and are overconfident. A useful lens if you evaluate small models on agentic code tasks.
  8. Keep Distributed Training Running with Automated Workload Recovery
    NVIDIA · blog post · 2 Nov 2026 · developer.nvidia.com
    An NVIDIA post on recovering automatically when distributed training jobs hit hardware or software failures. The short description does not say whether it works outside NVIDIA's DSX platform or with your training stack.
  9. Transformers Model Integration Contracts
    Hugging Face · blog post · 3 Nov 2026 · huggingface.co
    Title only: a Hugging Face post on model integration contracts in Transformers, probably about how model code is added to and checked in the library. Nothing in the record says what changes for people who train or evaluate with it.
  10. Diagnose Distributed AI Workload Failures with NVIDIA Attribution
    NVIDIA · blog post · 2 Nov 2026 · developer.nvidia.com
    An NVIDIA post on tracing cascading failures in distributed jobs, such as NCCL errors on other ranks, back to the GPU or fabric event behind them. Could save time on multi-GPU runs, but the description does not say what tool it is or what it requires.
  11. LifeVal
    OpenAI · blog post · 2 Nov 2026 · openai.com
    OpenAI's new benchmark of how well AI helps with everyday tasks such as finding childcare, managing finances and government paperwork. Context for evaluation; it is not a reasoning or code benchmark like the ones you report.

New papers

Read first (2)

  1. A Scaling Theory of Multi-Epoch Pretraining: Late-Time Overfitting and Ensembling
    Zhen Yang, Yidi Miao, Blake Bordelon · arXiv (cs.LG) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.03590
    Studies data-bound pretraining: on FineWeb, logit-averaged ensembles reduce late-training deterioration from repeated epochs and can beat wider or deeper single models at matched parameter-token budgets, and a mean-field theory gives compute-allocation laws at fixed data. Extends the Chinchilla-style allocation in your library to the repeated-data regime.
  2. Hinted Scaling Laws
    Suze van Adrichem, Luke Bailey, Rohith Kuditipudi et al. · arXiv (cs.LG) · 1 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.02270
    Hinted scaling laws give small models partial hints so their benchmark scores rise above zero: fit on runs up to 1e20 FLOPs, they forecast a 1e23 FLOP model's GSM8K accuracy within 3 points where a standard law was 13 points low. Speaks to the emergent-abilities and scaling papers in your library, and needs fewer test examples to rank close models.

Worth a look (8)

  1. A Fine-Grained Hierarchy and Benchmark for State Tracking in Language Models
    Jackson Petty, William Merrill, Ashish Sabharwal · arXiv (cs.AI) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.03094
    A four-level hierarchy of state-tracking problems with theory for transformers and Gated DeltaNet, and StateBench, on which no open or frontier model robustly solves even the soluble level. A new reasoning benchmark tied to code evaluation under variable assignment; Ashish Sabharwal from your library is an author.
  2. Is Your Long-Horizon Benchmark Really Deep? Measuring and Synthesizing Depth in Terminal Tasks
    Yu Jiang, Yue Huang, Hang Hua et al. · arXiv (cs.AI) · 1 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.01927
    Shows that turn counts in terminal-task datasets mostly reflect model habits and that 94% of solved synthetic tasks pass within three turns; DEPTH builds 105 deeper tasks that none of six models passes half of, and fine-tuning Qwen3-8B on its runs helps on two terminal benchmarks. Useful if you evaluate or train on agentic code tasks.
  3. Accelerating LLM Pretraining with Zeroth-Order Curvature on Idle Serving Compute
    Mingchuan Ma, Dongqi Cai, Daqian Yang et al. · arXiv (cs.LG) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.02688
    ZO-Curv uses idle forward-only serving hardware to estimate curvature with zeroth-order queries and refine first-order updates; at 770M parameters it reaches AdamW's 20K-step validation loss in 3.9 instead of 7.5 hours. Tested from 125M to 1.5B, so worth a look for your efficient-training work if you have idle inference GPUs.
  4. Tune the Baseline First: Most of Muon's Scaled Weight Decay Gain Is a Tuning Gap, but Its Small Real Advantage Grows With Model Size
    Esther Xin · arXiv (cs.CL) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.02529
    Tuning learning rate and weight decay jointly for both methods removes about 90% of Muon scaled weight decay's reported gain, though the small remaining edge grows with size and the best LR-WD product shifts with scale. A caution for optimizer comparisons in your scaling runs, though tested only on tiny MoE models (1.9M-15M active).
  5. Impossible Code, Barely Changed Score: A Replay Study of Judge-Based Evaluation
    Masoud Fetanat · arXiv (cs.SE) · 1 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.01572
    On 421 coding problems, GRPO-trained checkpoints wrote invalid Python 63-67% of the time, yet a strong LLM judge's Pass@32 fell only about 3 points. If you ever score code with a judge, the authors recommend also reporting the share of answers that pass a syntax check, a cheap addition to your code evaluations.
  6. Diagnosing Model--Item Interactions in Language Model Benchmarks with LSIRM
    Jinwen Luo, Han Jiang, Ziang Xiao et al. · arXiv (cs.CL) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.02432
    Item-response maps of 1,348 models on BIG-Bench Hard show nearby items often share the same correct answer, stronger when scoring by option labels than option text; removing this effect moved two near-tied models hundreds of ranks apart. Directly on the BBH benchmark in your library and on how you score reasoning evaluations.
  7. Controlling Sharpening in LLM Post-Training
    Simon Matrenok, Nicolas Menet, Nicolas Flammarion et al. · arXiv (cs.LG) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.03146
    Derives closed-form optimal policies for KL-regularized pass@k and max@k and shows standard @1 RL reaches them through prompt-adaptive regularization or reward transformation; KAPPO gives direct control over sharpening on math and code with single-completion training. A method to try if your post-training trades pass@k for pass@1.
  8. FLAMIA: FLAsh MIcroscaling Attention for Fast and Causally Safe Training on Blackwell
    Fedor Velikonivtsev, Mikhail Koltakov, Denis Kuznedelev · arXiv (cs.LG) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.03344
    FLAMIA, MXFP8 and mixed NVFP4/FP8 attention kernels for Blackwell that handle packed variable-length documents without repacking, speed attention forward plus backward by 1.06-1.42x on B200 and B300, and keep Llama-style pretraining stable. Worth trying if you pretrain on Blackwell GPUs.

On the edge (13)

  1. Scaling Makes It Possible: How Large Models Master Impossible Languages
    Yaning Jia, Chunhui Zhang, Chiyu Ma et al. · arXiv (cs.CL) · 1 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.02015
    Fine-tuning LMs of several sizes on impossible languages, billion-parameter models do about as well as on possible ones while million-parameter models degrade. A scaling result on what language models can learn, at the edge of your work.
  2. Meta-memorization and memorization scaling laws in transformers
    Alex Nguyen, Kenneth A. Norman, Gautam Reddy · arXiv (cs.LG) · 1 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.01564
    Reproduces both memorization scaling laws (capacity roughly linear in parameters, larger models memorize faster) on random binary sequences and traces the speed-up to a meta-memorization effect. Background for data repetition and memorization in your pretraining runs.
  3. DistDebug-Bench: Can LLM Agents Diagnose Bugs in Distributed Systems?
    Yuxuan Jiang, Chenglin Liu, John Rose et al. · arXiv (cs.SE) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.03523
    DistDebug-Bench, 120 real bugs from 11 distributed systems in 5 languages for agents diagnosing failures at the code level, with substantial gaps for current agents. A new code-agent evaluation, more systems debugging than the program-synthesis benchmarks in your library.
  4. How Does Chain-of-Thought Supervision Teach Models to Reason? A Theoretical Analysis
    Kaito Takanami, Cengiz Pehlevan · arXiv (cs.LG) · 1 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.01740
    A solvable model linking the amount and length of chain-of-thought training to overthinking at inference, with phase transitions and cases where more training CoT first worsens long-horizon error. Theory on linear regression and small attention models; context for CoT instruction tuning like Flan in your library.
  5. Test-Time Scaling for Open-Ended Reasoning at Small Model Scale
    Adam Y. Ahmed, Abdullah G. Elafifi · arXiv (cs.LG) · 1 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.02208
    Semantic-cluster voting helps 3B instruct models on math by 10 to 29 points but barely on open-ended text, because almost all samples fall in one meaning cluster; the collapse tracks instruction tuning rather than size. A result on what instruction tuning does to sample diversity.
  6. Library of Layers (LoL): Adaptively Ordering Transformer Layers at Runtime
    Abdellah Rahmani, Sean McLeish, Ang Li et al. · arXiv (cs.LG) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.03526
    Library of Layers keeps a shared set of transformer blocks and orders them per input at inference, spanning dense, depth-recurrent and mixture-of-experts trade-offs, and is competitive with those when pretrained on text. An architecture to know about; the abstract gives no model sizes.
  7. GatedV: Self-Gated Values for Transformer Language Models
    Li-Ming Yu · arXiv (cs.CL) · 1 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.02145
    Applying SiLU to the value projection lowers held-out perplexity by about 2% at 2.33M and 26M parameters with no extra parameters and about 0.7% more latency. A cheap tweak, tested only at very small scale.
  8. VisCodeBench: a new benchmark dataset for Text-to-Vis reflecting real-world practice
    Hy Nguyen, Thomas James Elton, Ella Wilson et al. · arXiv (cs.CL) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.02353
    VisCodeBench, 2,672 text-to-visualisation tasks run on Codex, Claude Code and LIDA: execution is near-solved but no system reproduces the reference data well. A narrower code-generation evaluation than yours; the full dataset is kept private to prevent training use.
  9. Not All Reasoning Generalizes: A Case Study on Learning Generalizable Reasoning Strategies in RLVR
    Ziqi Pang, Yu-Xiong Wang · arXiv (cs.LG) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.03627
    In a controlled equation-solving task, RLVR teaches step-by-step derivation in distribution, but under harder shifts the model falls back to brute-force enumeration; co-training on bijective inversion tasks restores it. Context for how reasoning post-training generalizes.
  10. Tracing the Scientific Discovery Capacity of LLMs to Their Training Data
    Arda Uzunoglu, Mahler Revsine, Michael Schatz et al. · arXiv (cs.CL) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.03617
    Varying how many facts appear only once in the training data, the authors find more such monofacts yield more LLM discoveries in materials science and regulatory genomics. A training-data result at the edge of your data-mixture work.
  11. Same Outputs, Different Scores: Auditing the Unchecked Graders of Multimodal LLM Benchmarks
    Ahmad Jarjis Hasan, Jonathan Lwowski · arXiv (cs.AI) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.03377
    Rerunning the official scorers of eight multimodal multiple-choice benchmarks, the answer-extraction rule alone moved accuracy by up to 22 points on identical outputs; the authors ask for a non-compliance rate and two scoring rules. Multimodal only, but the same parsing question may arise in your reasoning evaluations.
  12. RLVR Sharpens the Pencil, Not the Mind
    Shreeyam Bangera, Manya Gupta · arXiv (cs.LG) · 2 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.02574
    Tracking OLMo-2-1B across 13 RLVR checkpoints on GSM8K, first-try accuracy rises from 58.0% to 61.3% while 256-sample coverage was already 94.5%, and probes find no change in how the model represents reasoning mistakes. Evidence on what RL post-training adds to a pretrained model.
  13. SWE-Trail: History-Guided Trajectory Collection for Coding Agent Training
    Ziming Li, Lu Yu, Jiatan Huang et al. · arXiv (cs.AI) · 1 Nov 2026 · arXiv:2611.02074
    SWE-Trail uses earlier coding-agent trajectories to steer where new ones are collected, without showing them to the agent; a 124B student trained on them gains 2.33 and 1.60 points on SWE-bench Multilingual and Pro. On collecting code post-training data, at the edge of your instruction-tuning work.
