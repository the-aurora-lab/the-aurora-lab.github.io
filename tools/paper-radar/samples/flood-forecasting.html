Good to know (1)

  1. A Framework for Efficient Well Control Modeling in Machine Learning Surrogates
    NVIDIA · paper · 2 Nov 2026 · doi:10.2118/236261-ms
    From another field. An NVIDIA neural-operator surrogate for an oil reservoir simulator: one branch takes the spatial geology, the other the time-varying well controls, and a differentiable physics module keeps the outputs consistent. The same split (catchment properties vs. weather forcing) could emulate a distributed hydrological model per ensemble member, but nothing in it is tested on water.

New papers

Read first (1)

  1. Evaluation of transfer learning in hydrological forecasting using ensemble LSTM models: a statistical approach to uncertainty and accuracy
    Sami Miaari, Daniel Klotz, Stefan Kollet · Frontiers in Water · 2 Nov 2026 · doi:10.3389/frwa.2026.1896829
    100 LSTMs predict water table depth at locations left out of training, and the ensemble spread tracks RMSE and bias there, so it estimates skill where there are no observations. It is groundwater on simulated data, not streamflow, but the same check fits your LSTM models for ungauged catchments.

Worth a look (1)

  1. Deriving a Water Height Acceleration Threshold for Improved Flash Flood Alerts in the Upper Guadalupe River
    Meredith L. Swallom, Jason M. Dortch · GSA Today · 1 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1130/gsatg647a.1
    Derives a gauge-specific threshold on the acceleration of water height from a 2018 flood; in the July 2025 Guadalupe flood every gauge passed it before major flooding, upstream gauges about 5.5 h ahead. A simple gauge-data trigger you could test alongside your forecast warnings.

On the edge (10)

  1. Flood susceptibility mapping using ensemble machine learning models and SHAP analysis in the Shebelle River Basin, Somalia
    Radhwan A. A. Saleh, Ahmed M. Al‐Areeq, Abdikarim Moallim Mohamoud et al. · Geocarto International · 2 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1080/10106049.2026.2756764
    A stacking ensemble of gradient-boosted trees with SHAP maps flood susceptibility in the Shebelle basin, with Al-Areeq, an author in your library. Static susceptibility mapping rather than forecasting.
  2. A Comparative Machine Learning Assessment for Urban Flood Susceptibility Modeling and Future Flood Susceptibility Projections in Dhaka Metropolitan Area (DMA), Bangladesh
    Mahinoor Islam Tonmoy, Asib Ahmed · Sustainable Cities and Society · 1 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1016/j.scs.2026.108071
    Title only: compares machine learning models for urban flood susceptibility in Dhaka and projects it forward. Susceptibility mapping rather than forecasting, close to the Bangladesh flood papers in your library.
  3. Large‐Scale Mechanisms Associated With the Predictability of Summer Monsoon Rainfall Over Homogeneous Regions of India in the Latest Seasonal Prediction Models of NMME and IITM ‐ MMCFSv2
    Nanaji Rao Nellipudi, Prasanth A. Pillai, Anupam Hazra et al. · International Journal of Climatology · 1 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1002/joc.70656
    Scores NMME and IITM-MMCFSv2 seasonal forecasts of Indian monsoon rainfall by region; a best-model ensemble beats the multi-model mean in hindcasts, but once model choice is cross-validated its skill depends on the region. Context for seasonal outlooks; your forcing is shorter-range NWP ensembles.
  4. Physics-informed temporal convolutional network with auxiliary process-state trajectories for effluent quality prediction
    Yiting Li, Zongrui Huang, 张晓林 et al. · Water Research · 1 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1016/j.watres.2026.127262
    Title only: a physics-informed temporal convolutional network that uses process-state trajectories as auxiliary targets, for wastewater effluent quality. Extra physical targets could carry over to rainfall-runoff networks, but the record has no abstract to say how.
  5. Co-Occurrence of Extreme Precipitation, Sea Level and Wind: Statistical Perspective
    Kapil Chandak, Zheng Ren, Marc de Vos et al. · Journal of Marine Science and Engineering · 2 Nov 2026 · doi:10.3390/jmse14212056
    Fits trivariate copulas to 50 years of daily extreme rainfall, sea level and wind at Bridgeport and finds wind direction changes how strongly rain and sea-level extremes co-occur. Relevant only for basins whose outlets can flood from both.
  6. 3D Property-Level Flood Risk Visualisation for Urban Flood Risk Communication: A Case Study of Te Auaunga, Auckland
    Fatemeh Asghari kaleshani, Mostafa Babaeian Jelodar, Farzad Piadeh et al. · International Journal of Disaster Risk Reduction · 1 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1016/j.ijdrr.2026.106548
    Title only: 3D property-level flood risk visualisation for risk communication in Auckland, with Farzad Piadeh, an author in your library. Communication of risk, not forecasting.
  7. Polar front jet regulates tropical sea surface temperature impacts on South China extreme precipitation
    Xuefeng Liu, Zhiwei Zhu, Shaobo Qiao et al. · Communications Earth & Environment · 1 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1038/s43247-026-04194-4
    Traces the record April-May 2024 rain in South China to a Rossby wave train guided by the polar front jet, and finds the ECMWF model underestimated the rain because of its jet biases. Context on where ECMWF rainfall forcing can fall short in extremes.
  8. Theoretical framework for updating cross‐sections in river models using non‐standard information coming from crowdsourced images
    Muhammad Haris Ali, Thaine H. Assumpção, Andréja Jonoski et al. · River · 2 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1002/rvr2.70075
    Updates HEC-RAS river cross-sections from blockages seen in geotagged citizen photos, shown with hypothetical data on the Kifissos in Athens. A way to bring non-standard observations into a river model, though it changes geometry rather than assimilating states.
  9. Manhattan Pluvial Flood Simulations Under Two Extreme Rainfall Scenarios Show Similar Severity Patterns but Contrasting Peak Timing Across Districts
    Seokkoo Kang · Earth s Future · 1 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1029/2026ef008707
    Building-resolving shallow-water simulations of Manhattan separate when each district first turns hazardous from when its hazardous area peaks; under an Ida-like storm the first falls within 35 minutes across districts. Urban pluvial, not riverine, but the timing split bears on warning lead times.
  10. Integrated Hydrological-Hydraulic Modeling for the 2025 Jakarta Flood and Its Implications for Urban Resilience
    Muhammad Syahril Badri Kusuma, Firman Pratama, Faizal Immaddudin Wira Rohmat et al. · Results in Engineering · 1 Nov 2026 · doi:10.1016/j.rineng.2026.113930
    Title only: integrated hydrological-hydraulic modelling of the 2025 Jakarta flood; the record has no abstract to say what forcing or observations were used.
